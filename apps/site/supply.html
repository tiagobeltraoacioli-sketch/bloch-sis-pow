Supply — Tokenomics V4

100 billion, hard-capped.

Genesis-4 fixes total supply at 100,000,000,000 BLCH (TOTAL_SUPPLY_BLOCH). The cap is a consensus invariant: every node refuses a block whose cumulative issuance would exceed it, and no mechanism inside the protocol — no vote, no key, no governance path — can raise it. Said precisely: a hard fork adopted by every operator can change any rule; nothing short of that can change this one.

Genesis-4 mainnet has been live since 2026-08-13 21:31:19 UTC. The 100/21 split and the Genesis-3 carryover re-measurement below are both final and compiled into every figure on this page — there is nothing pending to caveat.

Vesting

Cliffs six months apart, on purpose.

The classic failure is the cliff wall — several buckets unlocking in the same month. Founder, team and VC cliffs are staggered so unlocks arrive as a stream, and every linear vest runs per-slot, with no monthly steps to game.

Emission

Forty years of smooth disinflation, then fees only.

The recommended curve declines 10% per year with no halving dates — a halving is a scheduled day on which every validator’s revenue drops by half at once, and marginal operators leave together. Annual issuance is 4.34% of total supply in year one, 2.85% by year five, 1.68% by year ten, and the forty-year total lands on the validator allocation with a fixed, irreducible residual of EMISSION_DUST_SAT (855,280 sat — 0.0086 BLCH, permanently unissued). After year forty, validators earn fees only.

Why the curve matters more than the vesting

Modelling the unlock schedule against the decentralisation gates showed that the emission curve, not the vesting schedule, decides whether the “no entity above 25% of active stake” gate can ever be met. A flat curve leaves validators at ~24% of circulating supply after ten years; the front-loaded curve passes 45% inside two. That is why the curve was chosen by the gate, not by aesthetics.

Say it before you are asked

The supply is concentrated.

The numbers, plainly

The largest carried-over address holds 17,046,829,380 BLCH — 93.94% of the carryover (LARGEST_CARRYOVER_ADDRESS_BLOCH / CARRYOVER_TOTAL_BLOCH) — liquid from slot 0. It belongs to the founder, who mined it on the same chain, under the same rules as everyone else. With the new vested grant on top, the founder’s combined position is 27.04% of total supply (FOUNDER_TOTAL_BLOCH, pinned by assertion at 2,704 basis points). Relabelling changes no balance, so the number is stated here rather than discovered later.

What bounds it — and what does not

Three rules push against it: a declining cap on the genesis validator cohort that reaches one third of consensus weight within a year — below the finality-stall threshold, so after year one the founder cannot halt Bloch alone; a 1%-of-active-stake ceiling per validator; and a churn limit that makes any large stake movement publicly visible for days before it lands. None of these can see who is behind an address. Anyone telling you a protocol rule solves concentration is selling something.
