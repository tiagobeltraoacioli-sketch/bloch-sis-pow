Protocol

No elliptic curves on the critical path.

That single constraint decides most of the design: signature sizes, the shape of the shielded pool, why hardware wallets do not work yet, and what a proof-of-stake committee can and cannot aggregate.

Signatures

Two lattice schemes, both mandatory.

Every spend and every block is signed with ML-DSA-65 and Falcon-1024, and a verifier requires both. An attack has to break two independent lattice constructions at once — and the price is paid in bytes, on every transaction.

ML-DSA-65

NIST FIPS 204, module-lattice based. The scheme most of the post-quantum ecosystem is standardising on.

Falcon-1024

NTRU-lattice based, compact signatures, a genuinely different mathematical assumption. Its floating-point signing path is the operational hard part — which is why it is paired, not solo.

What this excludes

No secp256k1, no BLS aggregation, no pairing-based anything. It also excludes today’s hardware wallets: none implements either scheme. That is stated as a limitation, not hidden.

Consensus today

Genesis-3: proof of work, merged with Bitcoin.

The live chain is SHA-256d over a GhostDAG, at a 30-second target, and it is merged-mineable with Bitcoin — one unit of ASIC work can secure both chains. It runs until height 50,000 and then stops, by rule.

Work

SHA-256d

The same hash Bitcoin ASICs already compute. Post-quantum by Grover’s bound: a quadratic speedup, priced in, not a break.

Merged mining

AuxPoW

A Bitcoin miner embeds a Bloch commitment in its coinbase and one solved header pays both chains. Hashrate is borrowed, honestly labelled as borrowed.

The caveat

51%-attackable today

Low hashrate, few nodes, unaudited. Security here is a trajectory, not a present-tense claim — and the trajectory ends at height 50,000 anyway.

Consensus next

Genesis-4: proof of stake, same signatures.

The design borrows the shape the market converged on — slots, epochs, LMD-GHOST fork choice, a Casper-style finality gadget — and replaces the cryptography underneath it. No BLS aggregation exists for lattice schemes, so the committees are sized for explicit signatures.

Slot

30 seconds. 2,880 slots per day.

Epoch

32 slots — 16 minutes.

Committees

A full epoch committee of 128 votes once per epoch and drives finality. A per-slot subcommittee of 8 exists only to give LMD-GHOST its fork-choice weight between boundaries.

Finality

A checkpoint justifies at ≥ 2/3 of the committee’s active stake (compared as 3·attesting ≥ 2·total — no rounding ambiguity) and finalises on consecutive justification. An inactivity leak starts after 4 stalled epochs.

Entry and exit

Deposits wait 8 epochs (~2.1 h) before the activation queue, which admits 4 validators per epoch; exits take 32 epochs (~8.5 h). An attacker with unlimited coins still needs publicly visible queue traffic, for weeks, to take a majority.

Status

Devnet. The node produces, attests and finalises across real processes — and has no persistent store, no gossip network, no transactions, no slashing pipeline and no RPC yet. Said plainly here so nobody has to discover it.

Privacy

Coherence: a shielded pool with no curves in it.

Zcash-style shielded pools rest on elliptic-curve zero-knowledge proofs, which is exactly the assumption this chain refuses. Coherence rebuilds the pool from hashes.

Commitments

SHAKE-256 everywhere

Note commitments, nullifiers and the incremental Merkle tree are all SHAKE-256 constructions. No trusted setup existed, so none can be betrayed.

Proofs

STARK / FRI

Spends are proven in a hash-based STARK (SP1/FRI) and verified as the raw FRI proof — never wrapped in a curve SNARK, which would reintroduce the assumption the pool exists to avoid.

The caveat

No privacy claim yet

Proofs are large, the prover is a scaffold, and the pool is unaudited. Until an audit says otherwise, Coherence is a design with tests, not an anonymity promise.
